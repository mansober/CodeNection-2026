# Santai — student survey (Google Form)

**10 questions. Under 2 minutes.** Built around your three areas:
problem and pain point · weaknesses of current tools · useful features.

**Do not collect email addresses.** Anonymous gets more honest answers about falling behind.

---

## Form title
**How do you actually manage your semester?**

## Form description
> A 2-minute survey by 4 APU students. We are building an app for students who join a lot of
> competitions and activities. We want to know if the problem we think exists is real.
> Anonymous — no names collected.

---

# PART 1 — Your week *(problem and pain point)*

### Q1. Outside of class, how many things do you commit to in a normal week?
*(clubs, competitions, part-time work, volunteering)*
**Type:** Multiple choice
- None
- 1 or 2
- 3 or 4
- 5 or more

> Splits your results into "active students" (3+) versus everyone else. If the active group answers
> worse on Q2 and Q4, that comparison alone proves you picked the right target users.

### Q2. Have you ever fallen behind in your studies because of activities outside class?
**Type:** Multiple choice
- Yes, more than once
- Yes, once
- No

> **The most important question in the form.** Becomes one line in your README:
> *"X% of students we surveyed have fallen behind because of activities outside class."*

### Q3. When your week gets too full, what suffers first?
**Type:** Checkboxes *(tick all that apply)*
- Sleep
- Assignments and studying
- Exercise or sport
- Time with friends or family
- My mood
- Nothing really suffers

### Q4. Do you usually know a week will be too packed *before* it happens?
**Type:** Multiple choice
- Yes, I can see it coming
- Sometimes
- No, I only notice when I am already in it

> Proves load is invisible until it hits — which is exactly what "see the cost before you say yes"
> is built for.

---

# PART 2 — What you use now *(weaknesses of current tools)*

### Q5. What do you use to keep track of your week?
**Type:** Checkboxes *(tick all that apply)*
- Google Calendar
- Notion
- Phone notes or reminders
- Paper planner or notebook
- A to-do app (Todoist, Tick Tick, and similar)
- Nothing, I keep it in my head
- Other

### Q6. Does what you use now tell you how *tired* a week will make you?
**Type:** Multiple choice
- Yes
- No, it only shows time and dates
- I never thought about it

> **Your competitor gap, in students' own words.** A calendar counts hours. It cannot tell you a
> week is mentally full.

### Q7. What annoys you most about what you use now?
**Type:** Checkboxes *(tick all that apply)*
- Takes too much time to set up
- I forget to open it
- It only shows time, not how hard things are
- It makes me feel guilty
- It is built for office work, not student life
- Nothing annoys me

> That fifth option is deliberate — it tests your claim that existing tools are not made for
> students.

---

# PART 3 — What would help *(useful features)*

### Q8. During study week, how much of the material feels like you are seeing it for the first time?
**Type:** Linear scale, 1 to 5
- 1 = I remember almost everything
- 5 = Almost all of it feels new

> If the average lands at 3.5 or higher, your flashcard feature has a number behind it.

### Q9. Which of these would actually be useful to you?
**Type:** Checkboxes *(tick all that apply)*
- Seeing how full my week is across time, mental effort, body and social energy
- Testing a new commitment before saying yes, to see what it does to my week
- A daily card from my own lecture slides, so I do not fall behind
- Rest suggestions that match what is actually draining me
- A pet that grows when I keep my week balanced
- Seeing which commitments I could move, drop or make lighter

> Whichever option wins is the feature you open the video with.

### Q10. In one sentence — what is the hardest part of managing your semester?
**Type:** Paragraph *(not required)*

> Your best quotes come from here, in language none of you would have written.
